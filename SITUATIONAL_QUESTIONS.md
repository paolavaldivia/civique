# Mises en Situation - Situational Questions

This document describes the new "Mises en situation" (Situational Questions) category created for exam preparation.

## Overview

The official civic exam includes both:
1. **Knowledge questions** (connaissances) - Published officially ✅
2. **Situational questions** (mises en situation) - NOT published officially ❌

Since situational questions are not published, we've created **35 realistic practice questions** based on French republican values, laws, and common scenarios immigrants might face.

## Purpose

These questions test your ability to **apply** French civic knowledge in real-life situations, helping you:
- Understand how laïcité works in practice
- Recognize and respond to discrimination
- Know your rights and responsibilities
- Apply republican values in daily life
- Navigate French institutions and services

## Question Format

Each question includes:
- **Realistic scenario** - A situation you might encounter
- **4 answer choices** - Including common misconceptions
- **Correct answer** - Based on French law and values
- **Detailed explanation** - Why this is correct and what law/principle applies

## How These Questions Help You

### Knowledge Questions Test:
- "What is laïcité?"
- "What does the Constitution say?"
- "When was this law passed?"

### Situational Questions Test:
- "Your colleague asks you to change your behavior - what do you do?"
- "Your child's teacher wears religious symbols - is this allowed?"
- "Someone discriminates against you - what are your options?"

**Situational questions are harder** because they require:
1. Understanding the principle
2. Recognizing when it applies
3. Choosing the appropriate action
4. Considering French legal and cultural context

## Key Themes Across All Questions

### 1. Balance of Rights
Many situations involve balancing:
- Individual freedom vs. collective interest
- Religious freedom vs. laïcité
- Freedom of expression vs. public order
- Workers' rights vs. business needs

### 2. Proper Channels
Questions emphasize using proper legal channels:
- Police (17) for crimes/emergencies
- Défenseur des droits for discrimination
- Labor inspection for work issues
- Dialogue before confrontation

### 3. Republican Values in Practice
Questions reinforce:
- Equality (égalité) - No discrimination, equal treatment
- Liberty (liberté) - Personal freedom within legal limits
- Fraternity (fraternité) - Solidarity, helping others
- Laïcité - Separation of state and religion
- Dignity - Respect for all persons

### 4. Common Misconceptions Addressed
- "I can impose my religious practices on others" ❌
- "Discrimination is okay in private business" ❌
- "Police can do whatever they want" ❌
- "I must accept all demands from authority" ❌
- "Only French citizens have rights" ❌

## Study Tips

### 1. Don't Just Memorize
Understand the **why** behind each answer:
- What French value does this protect?
- What law applies?
- Why are other options incorrect?

### 2. Think Like a French Citizen
Ask yourself:
- Does this respect laïcité?
- Does this treat everyone equally?
- Does this balance individual and collective rights?
- What would maintain social cohesion?

### 3. Know Your Resources
Memorize key contacts:
- **17** - Police/Emergency
- **3919** - Domestic violence hotline
- **Défenseur des droits** - Discrimination/rights violations
- **Préfecture** - Administrative issues
- **Mairie** - Local services

### 4. Practice Scenarios
For each question, imagine yourself in the situation:
- What would you instinctively do?
- What does French law require?
- What's the difference?

## Important Legal Concepts

### Must Know:
1. **Laïcité** - Not anti-religion, but separation of state and religion
2. **Non-discrimination** - Based on origin, religion, sex, disability, etc.
3. **Freedom of expression** - Strong but not absolute (no hate speech, defamation)
4. **Labor rights** - Strong worker protections in France
5. **Mandatory education** - Until 16, then formation until 18
6. **Assistance duty** - Legal obligation to help persons in danger
7. **Right to housing** - Droit au compte (bank account), housing protections

## Real Exam Tips

On the actual exam:
1. **Read carefully** - Scenarios may have subtle details
2. **Eliminate obviously wrong** - Usually 1-2 answers are clearly incorrect
3. **Look for French values** - The correct answer usually respects republican principles
4. **Avoid extremes** - Rarely is the answer "always" or "never"
5. **Think practical** - France values pragmatic, balanced solutions
